About

We started because nobody would sell us four cases

Marnwood Packaging Co. has been supplying compostable and recyclable packaging out of Hamilton, Ontario since 2021. Nineteen of us, two warehouses, and around 180 wholesale accounts.

The short version

Ivy Marnwood ran a bakery on Locke Street for nine years. Around 2019 their customers started asking what happened to the coffee cups, and the honest answer was that nobody at the bakery knew. So Ivy went looking for compostable packaging and hit the wall every small operator hits: the good stuff existed, but the distributors wanted pallet minimums and twelve-week lead times, and a bakery doing 200 covers a day has nowhere to put a pallet of cups.

What Ivy did instead was phone four other Locke Street businesses and split a pallet five ways. That worked well enough that people started asking to be included, and by the spring of 2021 the bakery’s back room was mostly other people’s cups. Marnwood was incorporated that June, largely so the bakery could have its back room back.

The splitting-a-pallet logic is still the whole business model. We buy at a scale that gets decent pricing and reasonable certification paperwork, and we break it down to the size an independent cafe can actually store and afford. That’s why our case counts are what they are, and why we’ve stayed at four product lines instead of chasing a catalogue.

Where we are

Head office and the main warehouse are at 412 Barton Street East in Hamilton — an old textile building with a loading dock that floods slightly every March. Everything for Ontario and points west ships from there.

In April of this year we opened a second warehouse on rue Saint-Patrick in Montreal, and signed our first Quebec accounts a few weeks later. It was overdue. We’d been trucking Quebec orders out of Hamilton and watching two days of transit turn into four whenever the 401 had a bad week. Quebec accounts now ship from Montreal, usually next day on the island.

Who works here

Nineteen people as of this spring:

  • Six in the Hamilton warehouse, picking, packing and folding sample boxes
  • Three in Montreal, running the new warehouse
  • Four on accounts and sales, split between Ontario and Quebec
  • Two on customer service, which is who answers the phone
  • Two on sourcing and quality, including the person who chases certification paperwork
  • One bookkeeper
  • Ivy, who still does the autumn price review by hand

Nobody here has a title with the word “officer” in it. If you call during business hours you will get a person, and there is a reasonable chance that person has personally packed one of your orders.

Staying in touch

We send a newsletter roughly monthly — restock dates, new SKUs, freight changes, and once a year the new price list. The customer list is up around 2,400 addresses now, which still surprises us given it started as a group email to four businesses on Locke Street. If you want to be on it, say so on the contact form.

What we’re not

We’re not the cheapest. A distributor buying containers of PLA-lined cups will beat our cup price and we won’t try to match it. What we do is stock four lines properly, tell you honestly what breaks down and what doesn’t, and answer the phone. For most of the cafes and bakeries we work with, that turns out to be the thing that mattered.
